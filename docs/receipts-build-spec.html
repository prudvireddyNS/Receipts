Product & design spec · v1 For Ragav · Aug 2026

Receipts

Rebuilding TrackBudget into something a 22-year-old opens on purpose.

Every colour, size, weight, spacing value, animation duration and line of copy is specified here, so that none of it has to be re-decided while you're building. That is the only reason this document is this detailed.

Ragav owns this product. Add features, cut features, reorder the phases, disagree with any of it, throw out the parts that don't survive contact with real code. This is the strongest default we could write — it is not a contract.

The one ask: change it deliberately, and in one place, rather than drifting away from it file by file. A design system only works if it's coherent, and which coherent system you land on matters far less than that it stays one. If you replace something here, write down what you replaced it with. That's the whole process.

The engine you already built is good and it stays. model/, data/ and sms/ are keepers — SMS ingest, SHA-256 dedupe, refund-netting, learned rules, the analytics pass. Roughly 1,000 lines of correct domain logic that took real work to get right.

The UI layer is what we're replacing. ui/TrackBudgetApp.kt is 1,674 lines holding every screen, sheet and helper in one file, and it renders an app that looks like accounting software. That file goes.

Where we are

Three things kill it for this audience

Not opinions — each maps to a specific thing on screen right now.

1. It's a shame engine

The hero number is “Left to spend today ₹264.16” sitting on a bar that drains. Every single app open delivers the same message: you are running out.

Shame is the shortest-lived motivator there is. It's the most-cited reason budgeting apps see roughly 60% abandonment inside 30 days — users disengage well before the behaviour becomes a habit. Apps that survive use building framing: progress made, next milestone, record accumulated.

The fix is not new maths. safeTodayMinor is a good number and we keep computing it exactly as-is. We change what it's called and what sits next to it.

2. Insights is a bank statement

The Insights tab currently ends on “Average confirmed debit ₹183 · Transactions tracked: 10” and then half a screen of nothing. Nobody has ever wanted the arithmetic mean of their debits.

Meanwhile Analytics.kt already computes projected spend, committed-vs-flexible split, category shifts against the previous period, top merchants, concentration share and recurring detection — and almost none of it reaches the screen in a form anyone would read. The data is there. The delivery is missing.

3. Nothing is shareable

Not one screen in the app is worth screenshotting, in a demographic whose primary mode of expression is screenshotting things. There is no artifact, no reveal, no moment worth sending to a group chat.

One more thing, and it shapes everything

The users are UPI-native Indians, not Americans, and that is a genuinely different animal. Bills, groceries and food eat 70%+ of Gen Z salaried spend. Quick-commerce spend runs about 3× that of older cohorts. 56% have missed a credit payment at least once. Rent and education payments run ~20% above other generations.

Translation: the defining spending behaviour is Blinkit at 1am, and the app currently has no opinion about it. It should have a very strong one.

The idea

Receipts

Gen Z slang: showing the receipts means producing the evidence. That is exactly what the SMS parser already does — it quietly collects proof of where your money went, without being asked. The name does the positioning for free.

Three nouns carry the entire product. Learn these; they are the vocabulary for the code, the UI and the copy.

  • 01Receipts — the transactions. Auto-captured, occasionally needing a nudge from you. This is Transaction; don't rename the class, just the user-facing word.
  • 02Drops — auto-generated insight cards, delivered like content rather than reporting. One fact, one number, one dry line. Share-ready by construction.
  • 03Stamps — collectible rubber-stamp achievements. Retrospective discoveries, not daily chores.

Plus Goals, which are money you're aiming at rather than money you've spent — a Goa trip, a laptop, a phone. Visually a goal is a perforated ticket stub that fills with ink as you save. The metaphor holds: a goal is a receipt you haven't earned yet.

The reframe that matters most

The single highest-leverage change in this document, and it costs almost no code.

Now: “Left to spend today ₹264.16” over a draining bar. Frame: depletion. You are losing.

New: “₹1,830 in — on pace” over a pace rail with a marker for where you'd be if you were perfectly even. Frame: record. You are accumulating evidence.

Identical arithmetic. dashboard() doesn't change by one line. We show spent-so-far as the hero instead of remaining, and we express safeTodayMinor as a position relative to an even-pace marker rather than as a shrinking allowance.

There are three pace states, and they are the app's only judgement. Note that none of them praise or scold — they report.

StateConditionColourLine
Underspent < 92% of even-paceUltramarine“under pace”
On92–108% of even-paceInk“on pace”
Overspent > 108% of even-paceChilli“ahead of pace”

“Ahead of pace” rather than “over budget” is deliberate. It's factually precise, it's not an accusation, and it leaves room for the user to decide whether they care.

Who this is for

There is no such thing as “the Gen Z user”

A 19-year-old on an irregular allowance from home, a 24-year-old two years into a salaried job, and a 22-year-old freelancer paid in lumps have almost nothing in common financially. Designing one home screen for all three produces something that fits none of them. This section is load-bearing — build it before you build anything in the Screens section.

We handle this with two onboarding questions, not a wall of settings. Both are changeable later, and both genuinely reconfigure the app rather than just re-labelling it.

Question one: money rhythm

This decides how budgetRange() behaves. Your Budget.period field already supports Week, Month and Custom, so two of these are nearly free.

RhythmWhoperiodWhat changes
MonthlySalaried. Money lands on the 1st."Month"Current behaviour. Period resets on a chosen day of month (add a resetDay field — not everyone is paid on the 1st).
WeeklyStudents on weekly allowance, gig workers paid weekly."Week"Already works. Make it a first-class onboarding option instead of buried in the budget sheet.
IrregularFreelancers, students on ad-hoc money from home, anyone whose income is lumpy."Rolling"New. No period at all. A rolling trailing 30 days. Pace is measured against your own trailing average, not a budget you set once and can't meet.

Irregular is the important one and it's the one you'd skip. A large share of Indian Gen Z has no fixed monthly income, and every budgeting app on the store silently assumes they do. Handling it well is a genuine differentiator and it's maybe 80 lines.

Not everyone's bank sends parsable SMS

The current app treats SMS as the main path and manual entry as a fallback, and it shows — manual add is a form with two text fields. But plenty of users bank somewhere your regexes don't cover, or have SMS permissions off for good reasons, or are on a phone where the messages just don't arrive.

The manual path must be as fast as the automatic one is invisible. That's what the amount-pad in the Screens section is for — a receipt added by hand should take under three seconds and zero typed words. If manual entry is good enough, SMS permission becomes a genuine convenience rather than a hostage negotiation.

Covering everyone includes disabled users

This is part of the same note and it's the part that usually gets dropped. It is not optional, and retrofitting it costs five times what building it in does.

  • 01Never encode state in colour alone. The three pace states each get a distinct word (under / on / ahead) and a distinct marker position. Around 1 in 12 men has some colour vision deficiency, and our under-state blue and over-state red are exactly the pair that fails. The word is the real signal; the colour is reinforcement.
  • 02Respect the system font scale. Use sp for all text, never dp. Test every screen at 200%. The hero number is allowed to shrink; nothing is allowed to clip or overlap.
  • 03Touch targets are 48dp minimum, including the swipe-card action buttons and the numpad keys.
  • 04Every icon-only control gets a contentDescription. The gear, the close buttons, the category marks (read the full category name, not “FD”).
  • 05Honour reduced-motion. Check Settings.Global.ANIMATOR_DURATION_SCALE; when it's 0, skip the number roll, the stamp press and the receipt-print animation. Show end states instantly.
  • 06Contrast: all body text hits 4.5:1 against its ground, all large text 3:1. Chrome yellow on Paper fails badly — that's why Chrome is only ever a fill with Ink on top, never text on Paper.
Design system

Paper, ink, and Indian signage

Every finance app is a dark glass slab. This one is paper — warm ledger stock, near-black ink, and the high-chroma palette of Indian shop signage: ultramarine, chrome yellow, chilli red. It is on-metaphor, it's culturally specific to the people using it, and it will not be mistaken for anything else on the store.

The app is light-first. That is a deliberate risk and I want you to hold it rather than quietly drift dark. A dark theme is specified below and must work, but light is the default and the one we design against.

Space, shape, elevation

Spacing is a 4dp scale: 4 · 8 · 12 · 16 · 24 · 32 · 48 · 64. Nothing between. Screen horizontal padding is 20dp everywhere, no exceptions, so every screen's left edge lines up when you swipe between tabs.

Three radii only: 0dp (rails, dividers, full-bleed), 4dp (cards, inputs, chips, stamps), 99dp (pace rail, pills, avatars). A 12dp or 16dp radius anywhere is a bug.

No drop shadows. Anywhere. This is paper, and paper doesn't float. Depth comes from three things only: a 1dp Rule hairline, a fill change to PaperRaised, and dashed RuleHard perforations. If you find yourself reaching for elevation or shadow(), use a hairline instead.

The perforation is the app's signature device — a 1.5dp dashed RuleHard line, 4dp dash / 4dp gap. Use it wherever a receipt would tear: between the total and the commentary on a Drop, between a goal's progress and its actions, above the tab bar on Today. Use it sparingly enough that it stays special — at most twice per screen.

Category marks

No emoji. Emoji as category icons is the single fastest way to make an app look generated, and it's in seven of the eight references we looked at. But custom illustration isn't realistic either.

So: a two-letter monogram — 20dp rounded square (4dp radius), filled with the category colour, two uppercase letters in Spline Sans Mono 700 at 9sp, in white or Ink depending on the fill's luminance. Cheap, consistent, and it looks like a deliberate system rather than a shortcut.

fun categoryMark(c: Category): String = when (c.id) {
    "food" -> "FD";  "groceries" -> "GR"; "shopping" -> "SH"
    "transport" -> "TR"; "bills" -> "BL"; "transfers" -> "TF"
    "medical" -> "MD"; "travel" -> "TV"; "rent" -> "RN"
    // ...one per category, always two chars
    else -> c.name.take(2).uppercase()
}

// Contrast rule — no guessing:
fun onCategory(color: Long): Color =
    if (luminance(color) > 0.55f) Ink else Color.White
Voice

Deadpan, never parental

The app states facts with a flat affect and lets the number carry the drama. It is dry, occasionally funny, and it never once tells you how to feel about your own money. This is where most finance apps for this audience fall apart, so the rules are strict.

  • 01No exclamation marks. Anywhere in the app. Not one.
  • 02No emoji in system copy. User-typed notes can contain whatever they like.
  • 03No praise and no scolding. Never “You're doing great”, never “Keep it up”, never “too much”, “overspending”, “you should”. Praise loops burn out in about three weeks and scolding burns out faster.
  • 04Second person, short sentences. Under 12 words where possible.
  • 05Humour lives in Drops only. Never in errors, settings, permissions or money-in confirmations. A joke next to a failure is hostile.
  • 06Numbers are never softened. ₹1,830 is ₹1,830, not “about ₹1.8k” — except in charts where space genuinely forces it.

Banned strings — grep for these before every release

Empty states

These matter more than you'd think — a new user sees three of them before they see any data.

  • No receipts yet: “Nothing yet. Pay for something and this fills itself in.”
  • Review queue empty: “Nothing needs you. Clean.”
  • No Drops yet: “Give it a week. Drops need something to notice.”
  • No goals: “Nothing saved for yet. What are you after?”
  • No stamps: “24 to collect. You'll get them by living your life.”
  • SMS permission refused: “Without SMS you'll add receipts by hand. That works, it's just slower.” — no guilt, no re-ask, no dark pattern

Wrapped

On the 1st, the closed month becomes a five-card vertical story: total spent → where it went → the one number that stands out → stamps earned → versus last month. Chrome on Ink, full-bleed, one number per card, swipe down to advance.

Every card exports as a 1080×1920 PNG via a Compose GraphicsLayer capture. This is the app's one genuinely viral surface — it's the reason anyone outside your friend group ever hears about it. Build it in Phase 4, but design every Drop card from Phase 2 onward as though it will end up here.

Insights

The Drop engine

A Drop is a pure function from transactions to an optional card. No state, no ML, no network. Most of them are ten lines because Analytics.kt already did the hard part. Build them as a list of rules and let the feed sort by score.

data class Drop(
    val key: String,          // stable — "1am-tax-2026-08" — used for dismissal
    val kicker: String,       // mono label, e.g. "The 1am tax"
    val figure: String,       // the big number, pre-formatted
    val line: String,         // one dry sentence
    val score: Int,           // 0..100, feed sorts desc
    val shareable: Boolean = true,
)

interface DropRule { fun evaluate(tx: List<Transaction>, a: SpendingAnalytics): Drop? }

A Drop only appears if it clears its floor — a Drop about ₹40 is noise. Floors are given below and are not negotiable; an app that says something every day about nothing gets uninstalled in a week.

Two ordering rules. Never show more than three Drops per day in Loud mode, three per week in Quiet. And never show the same key twice — that's what the dismissed_drops table is for. A Drop the user marks “Not useful” suppresses that rule for 60 days, not just that instance.

Never write a Drop that tells the user what to do. “Try cooking at home” is the exact moment someone uninstalls. Report the number; they are perfectly capable of drawing the conclusion.

Achievements

Stamps

Twenty-four collectible rubber stamps. The design brief for the whole system is one line: most stamps should be discovered, not pursued. Daily-streak mechanics are what produce the engagement fatigue that kills these apps — a thing you must feed every day becomes a thing you resent, then a notification you disable, then an uninstall.

So: only two of the twenty-four are streaks, both short and both non-punishing (breaking one loses nothing you'd earned). The rest you get by living your life and later finding out you got them.

Visually a stamp is a 1.5dp Chilli border, 4dp radius, Chilli text in Spline Sans Mono 700 uppercase, rotated between −4° and +4° (seeded from the stamp id so it's stable). Locked stamps are dashed RuleHard on Fade. The earn animation is a press: scale 1.6 → 0.92 → 1.0 over 380ms with a HapticFeedbackConstants.CONFIRM, then a 2s hold. Once. Never repeat it.

  • Long-press the hero → hold to reveal the same figure for the previous three periods. Release to return.
  • Pull down on Today → re-scan the SMS inbox.

period_snapshots is written once when a period rolls over. It's what makes Wrapped and every “versus last month” comparison cheap instead of a full-table scan, and it survives the user later editing old transactions.

Guardrails

Things that will quietly wreck it

Every item here is a real failure mode for this specific design, not general advice. When something looks off and you're tempted to fix it by adding, check this list first — the fix is almost always subtraction.

  • 01No shadows, no elevation, no blur. Paper doesn't float. Hairline, fill change, or perforation.
  • 02No Color(0xFF…) outside Tokens.kt. Add a lint rule if you can; grep for it before every release if you can't.
  • 03No unrestyled Material3 components. Default Button, Switch, TopAppBar, Card and FilterChip all carry Material's radii and elevation and will look imported from a different app. Wrap every one.
  • 04No emoji anywhere in the UI. Category marks are two-letter monograms. This one is worth being stubborn about.
  • 05No centred body text. Left-aligned, always. Centring is only for the amount on the Add pad and stamp faces.
  • 06No radius that isn't 0, 4 or 99.
  • 07No spacing value off the 4dp scale. If 14 looks right, you want 12 or 16.
  • 08No proportional figures. Every ₹ amount is Spline Sans Mono with tabular numerals, so columns of numbers line up. This is most of why the lists read as receipts.
  • 09No second accent colour. Ultramarine, Chrome and Chilli each have one job. Chrome never becomes “a nice highlight”; it means goals.
  • 10No perforation more than twice per screen. It's a signature, and signatures stop working when they're everywhere.
  • 11No notification that isn't user-initiated or genuinely new. No “we miss you”, no “you haven't checked in”, no streak-loss warnings. The two channels you have are enough.
  • 12No blocking the app on SMS permission. Manual-first must be a complete product on its own.
  • 13No network calls. The app stays local-first and offline. It's a genuine feature for a money app and it's worth saying so on the store listing.
  • 14No dark theme drift. When something looks wrong in light, fix it in light. Do not quietly make dark the default because it's easier.
